Offline world models hallucinate dynamics wherever the data is thin, and policies quickly learn to exploit those errors. Instead of forcing conservatism or gathering more demonstrations, we repair the dynamics model directly from human preferences over imagined rollouts, a setting we formalize as Dynamics Learning from Human Feedback (DLHF). Because naive DLHF demands an impractical preference budget, RENEW uses epistemic uncertainty to target labels where the model is most exploitable, improving sample efficiency and reducing exploitation on Jumanji and classic control tasks.
